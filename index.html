Brand narrative

Designing a festival that feels ceremonial, contemporary, and communal.

What this identity should evoke

NCAF should feel like a living exhibition: textured, warm, and celebratory. The interface must move beyond generic event branding and instead mirror the rhythm of processions, woven craft, stage curtains, and oral storytelling.

Design pillars

  • Dynamic asymmetry: layered compositions that feel curated, not boxed in.
  • Cultural warmth: cream surfaces, jewel tones, and handcrafted textures.
  • Editorial clarity: generous spacing and readable information flow.
  • Ceremonial motion: gentle upward reveals and elegant transitions.

Use this site for

  • Building campaign decks and presentations
  • Guiding social media creatives and caption cards
  • Creating event banners, countdowns, and announcements

Color philosophy

A palette with memory, prestige, and celebration built into every tone.

The brand palette balances earth, festivity, and artistic depth. Use these hues to create transitions and emphasis, not rigid dividers. Let color behave like stage light across textiles and paper.

Heritage Green

For anchors, navigation, and foundational storytelling sections.

Festival Orange

For announcements, key calls to action, and celebratory emphasis.

Archive Purple

For arts-led moments, cultural prestige, and secondary accents.

Canvas Cream

For the main reading surface so the experience feels soft, warm, and archival.

The no-line rule

Separate content through spacing, tonal shifts, and masks instead of hard borders. Let sections breathe and transition like unfolding festival cloth.

Gradient behavior

Hero moments, buttons, and covers should use green-to-deep-green or orange-to-purple blends to create a silk-like ceremonial finish.

Glass and texture

Floating UI can use translucent cream overlays with blur so color and movement show through without feeling cold or overly digital.

Typography

Tradition leads, clarity supports.

The typography system is a conversation between prestige and accessibility. Let serif headlines carry memory and ceremony, while sans-serif body copy keeps the archive usable.

How to build the feeling

  • Stack tonal surfaces instead of adding obvious outlines.
  • Use soft shadows as atmosphere, not as visible decoration.
  • Allow overlapping blocks, floating chips, and cropped shapes.
  • Prefer curved masks and wide whitespace over boxes and dividers.

Do's and don'ts

Keep the system expressive without losing clarity.

Do

  • Use wide spacing and overlapping layers to create poster-like depth.
  • Let serif headlines lead campaign moments and ceremonial statements.
  • Carry the palette into pubmats through gradients, masks, and tinted surfaces.
  • Use motion with restraint so content feels lifted, not busy.

Don't

  • Break sections with hard rules, boxes, or generic thin dividers.
  • Overload every asset with too many colors, chips, and text blocks.
  • Use cold white backgrounds or heavy black text that flatten the mood.
  • Replace the warm editorial feel with rigid corporate layouts.

Director's note: Think of each screen and pubmat as a cultural invitation. The identity should feel curated like an exhibit, festive like a procession, and warm like a shared story passed from one generation to the next.
